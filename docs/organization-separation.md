# Organization Separation — CTHC vs CACT

> Status: **draft** — the distinction below is a hard project rule; specific cross-linking language requires organizational confirmation in Phase 1.

This is a non-negotiable project constraint. Two distinct organizations must not be merged in branding, mission, voice, or content.

## Crimean Tatar Heritage Canada (this project — "CTHC")

- **Type:** cultural and educational organization.
- **Focus:** culture, traditions, history, language preservation, educational resources, community cultural events across Canada, translation of books into English, translation/subtitling of films and screenings for English-speaking audiences, oral histories, and preservation of photography, music, cuisine, literature, art, and heritage.
- **Voice:** warm, welcoming, educational, culturally specific, suitable for broad Canadian and international audiences.
- **Primary experience:** a living digital heritage museum.

## Canadian Association of Crimean Tatars ("CACT")

- **Type:** advocacy and human-rights organization (separate entity).
- **Focus:** human rights, political advocacy, engagement with the Parliament of Canada, protests and rallies, government relations, recognition of historical crimes, and public policy.
- **Voice:** advocacy-oriented.

## Rules for this website

1. **Do not merge identities.** Never present CTHC and CACT as one organization or blend their branding, logos, missions, or content.
2. **Keep the primary experience cultural and educational.** Advocacy is not CTHC's function.
3. **Historical and human-rights context is allowed where historically necessary** — for example, the 1944 deportation (Sürgün) as historical background — but framed as sourced, educational context, not as a call to political action, and clearly separated from CACT's advocacy work.
4. **Attribute clearly when referencing CACT.** If CACT's work is mentioned, name it as a separate organization.
5. **Route sensitive material for approval.** Any politically or emotionally sensitive passage is `requires-cultural-review` and needs organizational sign-off before publication.

## Cross-linking guidance (without brand confusion)

- Cross-links to CACT, if desired, belong in a clearly labeled context (e.g. a "Related organizations" or partners area), **not** woven into cultural content or primary navigation as if the same body.
- Use CACT's full name and a one-line description of its distinct focus so visitors understand these are different organizations.
- Do not reuse CACT's visual identity, campaign imagery, or advocacy language on CTHC surfaces.
- Open external links transparently and label them as leaving CTHC.
- Before publishing any cross-link, confirm with the organization how they want the relationship represented.

## Quick test before publishing

Ask: _Could a visitor come away thinking CTHC is an advocacy group, or that CTHC and CACT are the same organization?_ If yes, revise. The cultural/educational identity must remain unmistakable.
